     Mutual Identity-Signature Authentication, Responder Resend
                       draft-famed-h1-rr-00


Abstract

   This document specifies a two-message mutual authentication
   handshake in which the Responder transmits two copies of
   its signature on the ordered pair of participant identities
   in close succession.  The duplicate emission is intended to
   mitigate transient packet loss without introducing a
   separate retransmission protocol.

1.  Introduction

   This document specifies a two-message mutual authentication
   handshake in the H1 family.  The Responder emits two copies
   of its signature on the ordered pair of participant
   identities in close succession.  The Initiator processes
   the first received copy as in the conventional flow and
   SHOULD discard subsequent duplicates that carry the same
   signature within the same session.

2.  Terminology

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL
   NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED",
   "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted
   as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only
   when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.

   I             The Initiator party.
   R             The Responder party.
   ltkI, ltkR    The long-term signing private keys of I and R.
   pk(x)         The long-term signing public key derived from
                 the private key x.
   sign(m, sk)   Digital signature of message m under signing
                 key sk.

3.  Protocol Overview

       Initiator                                     Responder
       ---------                                     ---------
       construct mI = sign(<I, R>, ltkI)

                            ----- mI ----->

                                          (verify mI)
                                          construct mR

                            <----- mR -----
                            <----- mR -----

       (process first copy, verify mR, accept)

4.  Message Format

   InitiatorAuthMessage carries sign(<I, R>, ltkI).

   ResponderAuthMessage carries sign(<I, R>, ltkR).  Two copies
   of ResponderAuthMessage are emitted by the Responder per
   session.  Both copies carry an identical signature value.

5.  Processing Rules

   The Initiator transmits mI = sign(<I, R>, ltkI) in the
   conventional way.  The Responder verifies the Initiator's
   signature, computes mR = sign(<I, R>, ltkR), and emits two
   copies of mR on the network in close succession.  The
   Initiator processes the first received copy of mR as in
   the conventional flow and SHOULD discard any subsequent
   ResponderAuthMessage carrying the same signature within the
   same session.

6.  Security Considerations

   The duplicate emission of mR does not weaken any of the
   security properties of the run.  An adversary that already
   observes a single copy of mR already sees the same
   signature value, and the second copy provides no
   additional information.

   The handshake does not provide non-injective agreement on
   any session-level parameters in addition to the named pair.
   The signed plaintext consists only of the identities I and
   R; the duplicate emission does not strengthen this binding.

   Compromise of either party's long-term signing key allows
   the adversary to forge signatures under that key.  The
   protocol does not provide forward secrecy.
