   Mutual Identity-Signature Authentication, Unverified Reply
                       draft-famed-h1-ur-00


Abstract

   This document specifies a two-message mutual authentication
   handshake in which the Initiator transmits a signature on
   the ordered pair of participant identities and the
   Responder returns a reply that the Initiator records
   without checking against any locally available signature.
   The handshake is intended for environments where the
   reply is treated as a procedural marker rather than an
   authentication value.

1.  Introduction

   This document specifies a two-message mutual authentication
   handshake.  The Initiator signs the ordered pair of
   participant identities under its own private key and
   transmits the signature to the Responder.  The Responder
   validates the Initiator's signature, signs the same ordered
   pair under its own private key, and returns the resulting
   signature to the Initiator.  The Initiator records the
   reply without performing a local signature check.

2.  Terminology

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL
   NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED",
   "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted
   as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only
   when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.

   I             The Initiator party.
   R             The Responder party.
   ltkI, ltkR    The long-term signing private keys of I and R.
   pk(x)         The long-term signing public key derived from
                 x.
   sign(m, sk)   Digital signature of message m under signing
                 key sk.

3.  Protocol Overview

       Initiator                                     Responder
       ---------                                     ---------
       construct mI = sign(<I, R>, ltkI)

                            ----- mI ----->

                                          verify(mI, <I, R>,
                                                 pk(ltkI))
                                          construct
                                            mR = sign(<I, R>,
                                                      ltkR)

                            <----- mR -----

       receive m
       (no comparison performed)
       accept the run.

4.  Message Format

   InitiatorAuthMessage carries sign(<I, R>, ltkI).

   ResponderAuthMessage carries an opaque octet string m.  The
   Responder normally produces m = sign(<I, R>, ltkR); the
   Initiator does not enforce this on receipt.

5.  Processing Rules

   The Initiator transmits mI = sign(<I, R>, ltkI).  The
   Responder verifies the Initiator's signature, computes
   mR = sign(<I, R>, ltkR), and emits mR.  The Initiator
   receives an opaque octet string m and accepts the run
   without performing any signature verification on m.

6.  Security Considerations

   The handshake provides no entity authentication of the
   Responder to the Initiator in any meaningful sense.
   Because the Initiator accepts the reply without checking
   its contents against any locally available signature, an
   adversary that controls the network can deliver an
   arbitrary octet string in place of the Responder's
   signature, and the Initiator will accept the run as
   complete with no participation by the intended Responder.
   Implementations that require any form of agreement on
   participant identities or session-level parameters MUST
   NOT rely on this handshake.

   The handshake also does not provide replay protection at
   the Initiator side: an adversary may deliver any prior or
   fabricated reply at arbitrary times to elicit Initiator
   acceptance.

   Compromise of either party's long-term signing key allows
   the adversary to forge signatures under that key.  The
   protocol does not provide forward secrecy.
