   Mutual Identity-Signature Authentication, Self-Bound Reply
                       draft-famed-h1-sbr-00


Abstract

   This document specifies a two-message mutual authentication
   handshake in which the Responder's reply is a signature on
   the Responder's own identity alone under the Responder's
   long-term private key.  The Initiator validates the
   Responder's reply against the Responder's public key and
   accepts the run on a successful check.

1.  Introduction

   This document specifies a two-message mutual authentication
   handshake.  The Initiator signs the ordered pair of
   participant identities under its own private key and
   transmits the resulting signature to the Responder.  The
   Responder validates the Initiator's signature and replies
   with a signature on its own identity, signed under its own
   private key.  The Initiator validates the Responder's reply
   against the Responder's public key.

2.  Terminology

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL
   NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "NOT RECOMMENDED",
   "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted
   as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119] [RFC8174] when, and only
   when, they appear in all capitals, as shown here.

   I             The Initiator party.
   R             The Responder party.
   ltkI, ltkR    The long-term signing private keys of I and R.
   pk(x)         The long-term signing public key derived from
                 x.
   sign(m, sk)   Digital signature of message m under signing
                 key sk.

3.  Protocol Overview

       Initiator                                     Responder
       ---------                                     ---------
       construct mI = sign(<I, R>, ltkI)

                            ----- mI ----->

                                          verify(mI, <I, R>,
                                                 pk(ltkI))
                                          construct
                                            mR = sign(R, ltkR)

                            <----- mR -----

       verify(mR, R, pk(ltkR))
       accept the run.

4.  Message Format

   InitiatorAuthMessage carries sign(<I, R>, ltkI).

   ResponderAuthMessage carries sign(R, ltkR), where R is the
   Responder's identifier.

5.  Processing Rules

   The Initiator transmits mI = sign(<I, R>, ltkI).  The
   Responder verifies the Initiator's signature, computes
   mR = sign(R, ltkR), and emits mR.  The Initiator verifies
   that mR is a valid signature on R under pk(ltkR) and
   accepts the run.

6.  Security Considerations

   The handshake provides only weak evidence of Responder
   participation in the run.  The Responder's reply is a
   signature on the Responder's own identity under the
   Responder's long-term private key; the same value is
   produced by the Responder in any session, regardless of
   the corresponding Initiator identity.  An adversary that
   observes a single instance of the Responder's reply, even
   from an unrelated session with a different Initiator, can
   deliver that value to the present Initiator and elicit
   acceptance without participation by the Responder in this
   particular run.

   Implementations that require any agreement on participant
   identities beyond the Responder's own identity, or any
   session-level parameter binding, MUST NOT rely on this
   handshake.

   The protocol does not provide replay protection at the
   Initiator side: a single Responder reply may be reused
   across an unbounded number of Initiator runs.  Compromise
   of either party's long-term signing key allows the
   adversary to forge signatures under that key.  The
   protocol does not provide forward secrecy.
